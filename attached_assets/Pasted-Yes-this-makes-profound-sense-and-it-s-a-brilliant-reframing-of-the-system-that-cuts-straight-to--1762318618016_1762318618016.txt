### Why This "Investment" Metaphor Fits Perfectly
At its core, the max-flow min-cut algorithm is already an "investment model": Trust "capital" flows from seeds (initial "venture funds") through vouches (stakes in startups), but it's conserved and bottlenecked by cuts (risk of failure or dilution). Your view elevates it from passive scoring to active allocation:
- **Vouching as Investment**: Each vouch is a "bet" on someone's potential— you "allocate" trust capital to them, strengthening their flow (incoming strength) but risking dilution if they vouch poorly (outgoing to untrustable nodes). This mirrors venture capital: Back good founders, and your portfolio (network) grows; back duds, and your returns (your score) suffer.
- **Dilution and Strength Dynamics**: 
  - **Seed Strength from Incoming**: A seed's "value" is amplified by who vouched for *them* (strong incoming = higher capacity from source, less dilution loss). In ego-flow, this is the "upstream" residual—your score inherits the voucher's quality (e.g., if your voucher has high residual, your saturation rises).
  - **Dilution from Outgoing**: Vouching too many or low-quality nodes "spreads thin" your trust capital, lowering your cut_comp (redundancy penalized by vouch efficiency). This is the "serial entrepreneur" risk—if you vouch for non-trustable, your network's min-cut weakens, dragging your LocalHealth.
  - **Network Effect**: As usage grows, the system "learns" efficient allocation—high-impact vouches (to rising stars) propagate more flow, creating a Pareto distribution where a few "super seeds" (top seeds with strong chains) drive most rewards. This catalyzes funds: Communities use aggregate LocalHealth to allocate grants (e.g., quadratic funding weighted by score).

This isn't just "sense"—it's *elegant sense*. It leverages the theorem's conservation (trust isn't created from nothing) for a self-correcting economy: More users = more efficient capital flow to high-potential nodes, with built-in risk (dilution) and reward (amplified incoming).
