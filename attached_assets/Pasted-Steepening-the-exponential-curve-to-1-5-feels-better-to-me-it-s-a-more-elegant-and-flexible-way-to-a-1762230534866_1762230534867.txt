### Why Steeper Exponential (1.5) Over Raising Baseline (to 10)?
Both options aim to "stricten" by pulling down low/mid-range scores (e.g., 1-5 vouches from 12-60 to 5-45), creating room for higher discrimination at the top (e.g., 10 vouches at 85, not maxed). But raising the baseline is a blunt tool—it assumes "10 is healthy" globally, which doesn't adapt to graph topology (e.g., a dense 3-vouch ego might be "healthy" as 5 sparse). Exponential scaling is smoother: It applies diminishing returns naturally, rewarding incremental improvements without hard cutoffs.

- **Pros of Steeper Exponential (1.5)**:
  - **Natural Spread**: Exponents amplify differences exponentially—low saturation (e.g., 0.2 for 1 vouch) gets squashed hard (0.2**1.5 ≈0.089), while high (0.8 for 4 vouches) stays strong (0.8**1.5 ≈0.715). This gives a wider 5-100 range without flattening the top.
  - **Graph-Adaptive**: Doesn't rely on a fixed "healthy count" (like 10)—scales with actual saturation/redundancy from the graph (vouches, depth, density).
  - **Incentive-Aligned**: Makes "good" (3-5 vouches) feel achievable (24-45 pts), but "excellent" (10+ with depth) earned (85 pts)—encourages building quality networks, not just volume.
  - **Simple to Tune**: Change 1.5 to 1.3/1.7 for finer control; no redefining baselines.

- **Cons of Steeper Exponential**: Slightly more "punitive" at low end (1 vouch=5 pts feels harsh, but signals "build more trust"). Math is non-linear, harder to explain intuitively.

- **Pros of Raising Baseline to 10**: Linear and easy to understand ("aim for 10 vouches for max flow"). Directly addresses "easy max" for small vouch counts (1=6 pts).
- **Cons of Raising Baseline**: Still arbitrary (why 10, not 8 or 12?); doesn't handle graph quality (10 shallow vouches =60 pts, same as 10 deep). Compresses mid-range (5=30 pts, 10=60—linear jump, no nuance).

Overall, 1.5 exponential feels "better" for a trust system—it's principled (diminishing returns mirror real rep building) and avoids new magic numbers (builds on your existing saturation/redundancy as 0-1). Baseline raise is fine as a quick patch, but exponential scales better long-term.
