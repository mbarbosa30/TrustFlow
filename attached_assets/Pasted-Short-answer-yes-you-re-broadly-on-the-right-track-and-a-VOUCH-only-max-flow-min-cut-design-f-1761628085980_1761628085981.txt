# 1) What Levien’s/Advogato metric actually does

* **Node capacities by seed distance.** Each user (x) gets a capacity (c_x) that **decreases with distance from seeds** (Advogato used 800, 200, 50, …). 
* **Node splitting + sink edge.** Each node is split (x^{-}!\to x^{+}). There’s a **capacity-1 edge to the sink** (acceptance unit), and **(x^{-}!\to x^{+})** gets the remaining capacity. 
* **Endorsements become edges.** A certification (A\to B) is an edge (A^{+}!\to B^{-})** with high/“infinite” capacity in the classic write-up. (Practice varies; finite is fine.) 
* **Compute max-flow.** Using Ford-Fulkerson/Dinic; **a user is accepted iff there’s flow from (x^{-}) to the sink**. 
* **Attack-resistance bound.** The number of bad nodes accepted is bounded by a function of **confused** nodes’ capacities; i.e., the attacker must recruit real users, not just spin up sockpuppets. 

# 2) Where your VOUCH-only design matches (good)

* **Same graph skeleton.** You’re using node-split, a **1-unit sink edge (acceptance)**, and a **seeded flow**—all exactly per Advogato. 
* **Single tier is OK.** Levien used three *passes* (apprentice/journeyer/master) historically, but the **core theorem** (max-flow ↔ min-cut) doesn’t require tiers. Your “VOUCH = capacity 1.0” edges are fine.
* **Cut-based acceptance.** Your policy “min-cut ≥ 2 and seed-coverage ≥ 2” tightens acceptance beyond Advogato’s “flow ≥ 1,” improving robustness in small graphs while staying consistent with the theory.

# 3) Classic pitfalls called out in the literature—and fixes

**Pitfall A: Distance-inflation attack.**
Ruderman’s comparison notes a weakness: because **(c_x)** depends on **distance from seeds**, an attacker who shortens distance for a compromised node can **inflate its capacity** and then “waterfall” trust to many bad nodes. 

**Mitigations you should adopt:**

* **Epoch-lagged distances.** Compute (d(x)) and (c(d)) from the **previous epoch’s accepted subgraph**, not the live endorsement graph, so attackers can’t raise capacities inside the current run. (Publish this rule in `params.json`.)
* **Gentler capacity schedule.** Use a **shallower decay** (e.g., 800, 240, 96, 48, 24…) to reduce the “step-function jackpot” from moving one hop closer, or cap **per-node forward capacity** with a low ceiling after distance 2–3.
* **Dual constraints (you already planned):** require **min-cut ≥ 2** *and* **seed-coverage ≥ 2**. That makes distance-shortening alone insufficient.

**Pitfall B: Seed proximity sensitivity.**
The closer a confused node is to seeds, the more damage it can do. (Ruderman points this out explicitly.) 

**Mitigations:**

* **Seed diversity check.** Your **seed-coverage ≥ 2** rule directly dampens single-seed blast-radius.
* **Seed saturation guardrail.** Track max share of outflow from any single seed; if it spikes, reduce that seed’s effective cap next epoch (publish in dashboard).

**Pitfall C: Public social pressure.**
Advogato’s public certifications created perverse incentives (vouching friends at top tier). Social, not algorithmic—but real. 

**Mitigation:** You’ve already simplified semantics to **VOUCH only** and plan an **append-only, signed transparency log**. Keep the EIP-712 text very explicit (“public & permanent”).
